Design Process

In summing up my impressions of the creation process, I can distinctly divide it into three core phases: first, capturing and editing thematic photography for my Audiowalk narrative; second, recording the voiceover, editing the audio, and layering background ambient music and sound effects; and finally, designing the UI and UX framework for the digital interface.

To start from the beginning, I initially envisioned a dark, low-key color palette — evoking a night scene pierced by only one or two isolated light sources situated deep within the darkness. My goal was not to create something explicitly terrifying, but rather to craft an atmosphere that feels ambiguous and obscure. This subtle feeling of the unknown is precisely what makes the environment feel disturbing. 

Practically, the workflow began with capturing photographs within the location, documented sequentially from the beginning of the path to its very end. It was mutually agreed upon with the model that these specific portraits would be utilised for the project. Visually, I aimed to achieve an unsettling, ghostly effect within the images, introducing translucent artefacts and semi-transparent layers that leave the viewer slightly uncertain about what is truly unfolding in the frame.

There is a distinct sensation when you feel as though you are being watched, or as if something is about to emerge from the darkness. It is a feeling where your rational mind knows nothing will happen, yet your fear brings every latent monster to life, leaving you entirely unsure of how to react: whether to freeze, run, or attack. This choice paralysis is genuinely one of the most harrowing experiences when navigating a dark, abandoned space where no one else is present.

Once the imagery was finalised, it was time to prototype the first design concepts for my website. Initially, the layout looked completely different from what you see now. Honestly, that first iteration was one of my personal favourites, although thematically, it did not quite capture the exact mood I was aiming for. 

After developing a few alternative design options, I attempted to translate them into clean code within VS Code. At that moment, my creation process slowed down from running to barely crawling. I suddenly realised how limited I was by my current technical skills relative to what I actually envisioned. I found myself doubting how much I could accomplish and whether I was capable of executing it all before the final deadlines. I ended up starting over from scratch one or two more times because none of the ideas were functioning the way I imagined. Eventually, the process shifted from crawling to freezing entirely, prompting me to pivot and turn my attention toward recording my story.

Working on the audio tracks went much faster and easier after weeks of stagnation with the website development. I spent two consecutive days in our VK Raum, focusing initially on the recording sessions and later on editing and exporting the final versions for each of my Audiowalk stations. Those weekends carried a very special, focused vibe; I genuinely enjoyed myself and the creative process, even though it meant returning home at midnight.

The final days leading up to the submission were undeniably stressful, as I had to race against time to complete the website's frontend. I give the absolute most credit for this difficulty to JavaScript. But hey, I did it. My website was fully functional and completed, and it was later showcased among the other student websites during the Rundgang exhibition!

While this specific documentation covers my personal website, we also had to collaborate in small teams to build a centralised, general Audiowalk hub website, which serves as a gateway to each student's personal project. I was placed in the poster design team. We worked in close alignment with the coding team to ensure that the visual identity of our printed posters and the web layout were completely on the same page. Personally, I found the communication between our teams to be highly effective, and my group successfully designed two distinct variants of beautiful posters. They were proudly hung everywhere possible across the campus for the Rundgang!
